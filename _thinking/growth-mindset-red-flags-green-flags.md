From [Logan Ury](https://www.loganury.com/)'s newsletter.

## My key points

- Take responsibility: when things go wrong; for your part in past relationships
- Work problems instead of giving up: tough projects; conflict
- Ask for help when stuck
- Interested and curious in new things and people and skills, being a beginner again
- Reflective and self-aware

## Raw notes

Behaviours that reveal a growth mindset.

### How they handle challenges

- **They take responsibility when things go wrong:** “When something fails they do not blame external factors, they wonder what they could have done better.”
- **They feel control over their circumstances:** “Here’s an example of a fixed mindset red flag: In a past relationship, my partner constantly talked about how he ‘could have’ had a better career if only people recognized his talent, or how he ‘could have’ been so much more successful if the environment had been better. There was no talk of his own actions and choices, and no interest in trying again.”
- **They own their part in past relationships:** “Have they grown or learned something from past challenges with partners, friends, and coworkers? Or do they trash talk? Is the other person in their stories always the bad guy?”
- **They solve problems instead of throwing up their hands:** “My ex is a great example of someone with a fixed mindset. The night before we left on a trip, our flight got rescheduled to be a day later. He immediately started complaining about how this was going to ruin the whole vacation. I told him I’d call the airline (which he said was a waste of time) and was able to change our flights to be only a few hours after the original one. I was so frustrated that he wouldn’t try to figure it out with me. I hope to find the opposite trait in my next partner.”
- **They seek advice when stuck:** “People who ask for feedback or insight from experts are open to improvement. If someone is struggling and they actively seek out recommendations from other people, it shows they believe improvement is possible.”
- **They start (and complete!) tough projects:** “I like to observe how they handle something like a DIY home improvement. When something goes wrong (and it always does!), do they get frustrated and give up, or do they try again? Do they ask for help? Do they laugh it off when things don't go according to plan?"

### How they approach new things

- **They’re open to new experiences:** “They’ll order something off the menu that they’ve never tried before. They are excited to try new activities.”
- **They’re not afraid to be “bad” at something:** “They’re comfortable being a beginner. They’re up for something like a dance class even if they aren’t good at dancing.”
- **They learn new skills or pick up new hobbies:** “They do things just because they enjoy them, even if they’re not an expert. I just went on a date with a woman who showed me pictures of her lopsided pottery. She said she didn’t care what it looked like, she just loved the way it felt to make something. I thought that was so attractive!”

### How they communicate

- **They are curious:** “I’ve noticed people with a growth mindset are curious about many things, not just their own job or hobbies, but about everyone else’s as well. They ask questions about people’s passions and about how they spend their free time. It’s refreshing to meet someone who is just generally inquisitive.”
- **They look inward:** “They tell you about their ‘a-ha!’ moments and they talk about their own self-reflections.”
- **They can handle conflict:** “When you disagree with them, they treat it as another perspective to dig into rather than a rejection. If you tell them that something upset you, they treat it as helpful information about your preferences instead of feeling attacked.”
