## AI features

- **Collapses our knowledge, not expands it**
    - Entrenching orthodoxies, not widening views
    - Hides (in the output) or removes (in the input) outliers
    - Closes, not opens
- **Reinforces dominant cultural patterns and ideas, not fixes them**
    - White, Western, English language, recorded knowledge, not all of human knowledge
    - Wikipedia version of reality (rational discourse of educated parts of society), not real and messy reality
- **Biased (data), not impartial**
    - Humans (all the way down), not machines
- **(Sufficiently advanced) Technology, not magic**
- **Automation, not intelligence**
- **Optional, not inevitable**
- **Mode amplification, not choosing, selecting, deciding**
- Collapsing and presenting as one definitive answer, not offering options and perspectives
- Slop, not special
- **(Focus on) Current harms, not speculative risks**
- Popular, not perfect
- Simple and singular, not complex and multiplex

## Some concerns

- Quick fixes rarely are
- **The iron triangle still applies**
- **Look at the previous magic technology: phones and social media. Have they made the world a better place.**
- **Produces higher quantity, lower quality**
- **We don't learn by reading answers, we learn with effort**

### Addressing concerns

- Back to basics: what are the building blocks of quality
- Be more clear on our difference point
- Light touch: what's the simplest, softest, solution?
- What's our goal?

## Objections

- Not using it is slow and difficult
- "Everyone else is doing it" tends not to age well

## Stances

- **Think more broadly**
- **Think in systems**
    - Look for causes, not villains
    - Look for systems, not heroes
- **We live in a tech bro, technoableist, world**
- **Start with the problem, not AI as a solution**
- **Understand the harms and costs**
- **Clarify what's being automated**
- Use our existing rules, ethics, laws
    - It's just tech, not magic
- Opt in, not opt out
    - "If we use it", not "when we use it"
    - **Optional, not inevitable**

## Some big questions

- What are the dangers?
- What is it not?
- What are the alternatives?
- What's the sales pitch?

## Rebel tactics

- **Keep it short, funny, useful**
    - Funny, ridiculous, retorts (e.g. microwaves)
- **Be an exemplar of your convictions. Show, don't tell.**
- **Use existing tactics, strategies, laws, rules**
- **Use but/and**
- **Use the Slow technique**
    - **Suggest alternatives to what's wrong**
    - **Draw from time-tested solutions**
- **Ask for specifics, details**
    - "We'll fall behind!" How, exactly? Do we need AI for that?
- Make the enemy live up to their own book of rules
- **Let them trip over the truth**
    - Find the telling details
    - Give a dramatic demo
- Appeal to identity
- Create surprise by breaking people's guessing machines
