Notes from [Don't Call It Art by Austin Kleon](https://austinkleon.com/dont-call-it-art/).

## Raw notes

- (Blackout poetry) When the future comes, all experts becomes students

### 1. Throw Out The Instructions

#### Don't Call It Art

- ... **if you don't call it art, you take all the pressure off. Now you can just make stuff.**
- "Were we to meet this figure socially, this accusatory character, this internal critic, this unrelenting fault finder, we would think there is something wrong with [them]."
- Luckily art critics are only interested in art. If you don't call it art, the critics won't care what you're doing, and they'll leave you alone. Now you can do whatever you want.

#### It's Better When You Don't Know

- (Blackout poetry) Not knowing is the first step
- Matrix: Unintended/Deliberate vs Imperfection/Perfection
    - Deliberate Perfection: Boring mastery
    - **Deliberate Imperfection: Punk rock, chance operations, wabi-sabi, Navajo rugs, etc.**
- People who are able to stay creative their whole lives know that the minute they start knowing, it is time to go back to not knowing.

#### Figure It Out For Yourself

- Loop: Mess around 🔁 Find out
- **What we learn on our own goes deeper and sticks with us longer than what we've been taught.**
- When we're forced to learn things on our own, we make our own discoveries.

### 2. Don't Take Things too Seriously

#### Pretend It's A Comedy

- **"One of the symptoms of approaching nervous breakdown is the belief that one's work is terribly important." - Bertrand Russell**
- "Whatever inspiration is," said the poet Wisława Szymborska, "it's born from a continuous 'I don't know'"
- Anyone who's spent time with little kids knows that **if they ask you "Why?" more than two or three times–which they will!–the world suddenly becomes very strange and unknown to you.**
- "The 'silly question' is the first intimation of some totally novel development." - Alfred North Whitehead
- The Five Whys (lead to) The universe becomes mysterious

### 3. Give Yourself Time, Space, and Materials

- **Make a space. Fill it with materials. Find time to go there.**
- **I schedule playtime for myself.**
- It helps to have a space where you can really make a mess, leave it, and come back to it tomorrow.
- The things we give ourself to play with shape the kinds of ideas we have.
- We need tools to do work, but we need *toys* to really be at play. **Your tools should feel like toys that you can play with.**
- ... if you put any old toy away for a while and then bring it back, it becomes a new toy.
- Nothing makes us want to play like a new toy.

### 4. Permission To Be Bad!

#### Do Something you Shouldn't

- There's something about ***mischief***&mdash;goofing off; getting into trouble; dodging responsibilities&mdash;that **seems to activate the creative spirit**.

#### Make A Big Pile Of Imperfect Things

- It wasn't about the drawings, it was all about the *drawing*.
- When you're not worried about wasting you precious supplies, you don't have to worry about being perfect.
- "It seems that while the 'quantity' group was busily churning out piles of work&mdash;and learning from their mistakes&mdash;the 'quality' group sat theorising about perfection, and in the end had little more to show for their efforts."
- "The finished pieces that we share&mdash;they're dependent on these messy piles of imperfection"

### 5. Believe In Magic

#### Dress-up and Silly Rituals

- **You can establish rituals to help ease these transitions. The sillier the rituals the better.**
- Try playing dress-up! **A costume is a powerful device**.
- **An actor uses props to get into character. So can you.**

#### Messages from the universe

- **For the very young child, almost everything is *alive***, and children ascribe their feelings and thoughts to all sorts of inanimate objects.

### 6. Think Outside Your Head

- When we grow up, we think learning and thinking happens in our heads.
- ... like children, **we do some of our best thinking *outside our heads***.

#### Listen To Your Body

- (I like to keep a Rubik's Cube on my desk for when I get bored)

#### Work With Your Feelings

- "I keep thinking I shall have no more to say," said philosopher Mary Midgley, "and then finding some wonderfully idiotic doctrine I can contradict. She admitted it was "a negative approach, as they say, but one doesn't seem to run out."

### 7. Problems of Output are Problems of Input

#### No Input, No Output

- **When I stall out, it's time to start taking things in.**

#### Know What You Like

- I often get the most blocked when I lose track of what is it that I really like.

#### Copying Is How We Learn

- ... you start out copying your favourites, you move into parodying them, and eventually transform them into something of your own.

#### Go To The Library

- At the library, you aren't limited by age or ability. You can borrow books from any section.
- Be like the librarian: do not spend time judging yourself. Simply give yourself time, space, and resources to grow.

### 8. Borrow A Kid!

#### Visit The Children's Section

- In her book *Why You Should Read Children's Books, Even Though You Are So Old And Wise*, the author Katherine Rundell ...
- Rundell emphasises that reading kids' books is not about hiding or escaping the adult world, but seeking out new things within it.

### 9. Nothing is wasted

#### Creativity is the Residue of Time Wasted

- **To waste time in the modern world is considered to be the greatest of sins, and idleness is looked upon with suspicion.**

### 10. You Don't Need a Vision

#### Put The Future Behind You

- The past had already happened, so it is in front of you where you can see it;<br>The present is the sliver of now where they meet;<br>The future has yet to happen, so it is behind you where you can't see it.
