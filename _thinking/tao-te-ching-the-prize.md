## Whittled notes

**Don't think lightly of battle. Hold on to, keep, your compassion.**

- The prize is compassion.
- Thinking lightly of battle, engaging an enemy, makes us lose our compassion.
- Attacking what yields means throwing away compassion.
- Thinking your enemy is evil destroys your compassion, making yourself an enemy.
- The side with compassion prevails.
- The side that takes no delight in battle prevails.

## Gathered notes

- To attack what yields is to throw away the prize.
- UKLG note: The prize thrown away by the aggressor is compassion. The yielder, the mourner, keeps that prize. The game is loser take all.
- Thinking little of battle brings us closer to losing what we value.
- Underestimating your enemy means thinking he is evil. Thus you destroy your three treasures and become an enemy yourself.
- Note: the three treasures: simplicity, patience, compassion.
- There is no greater disaster than engaging an enemy lightly. Doing this costs us everything we value.
- Yes, when armies do engage it is the side with compassion that prevails.
- If two forces are about equal in strength, the one that takes no delight in war wins.
- Therefore when armies are raised and issues joined it is he who does not delight in war that wins.

## Raw notes

### 69. Using Mystery (UKLG)

> The expert in warfare says:<br>
Rather than dare make the attack<br>
I'd take the attack<br>
rather than dare advance an inch<br>
I'd retreat a foot.

> <strong>Nothing's worse than attacking what yields.<br>
To attack what yields is to throw away the prize.</strong>

UKLG note: The prize thrown away by the aggressor is compassion. The yielder, the mourner, keeps that prize. **The game is loser take all.**

### 69. Caution (BH)

> No calamity is greater<br>
Than the results<br>
Of thinking lightly of battle.<br>
Thinking little of battle<br>
Brings us closer to losing<br>
What we value.

## 69 (SM)

> There is no greater misfortune<br>
than underestimating your enemy.<br>
Underestimating your enemy<br>
means thinking he is evil.<br>
Thus you destroy your three treasures<br>
and become an enemy yourself.

Note: the three treasures: simplicity, patience, compassion.

## Sixty-nine (JP)

> There is no greater disaster<br>
than engaging an enemy lightly.<br>
Doing this costs us everything we value.<br>
Yes, when armies do engage<br>
it is the side with compassion that prevails.

## Chapter 69: On War (KL)

> The worst military error is underestimating the enemy, which also cost me my dearest treasures.<br>
Thus, if two forces are about equal in strength, the one that takes no delight in war wins.

## Chapter 69 (AW)

> Now the greatest of calamities is to attack and find no enemy,<br>
I can have no enemy only at the price of losing my treasure.<br>
Therefore when armies are raised and issues joined it is he who does not delight in war that wins.
