Note: I still say "scrum master", even though I often mean "agile coach".

## Scrum master generally

Focused on helping the team do their best work.

- Facilitating the events ("ceremonies"), and keeping them useful and on time
    - standsup, planning, retros
    - these can be treated as admin, or they can be useful
    - the team can and do do these themselves in some places
    - some places (like govt and corporates, generally places doing SAFe) use all the words but miss the meaning
- Shit umbrella / impediment-remover
    - keeping bullshit away from the team so that they can do the work
    - either removing roadblocks, or helping the team do it themselves, depending on the org and team
- Coaching on agile stuff and more generally
    - whether this can be useful in practice depends on the org
- Cheerleading agile things more generally
    - Really depends on the org
    - More "agile coach" than "scrum master"
- Product stuff (like backlog refinement, goal and direction), depending on the Product Owner
    - Sometimes writing user stories, even though that's really a Product thing
- Jira admin stuff, assigning work, reporting
    - Shouldn't really be part of the job, but often is
    - Orange flag
Same caveat — this is material to think with, not a conclusion.

## The modern, more delivery-focused, version

Delivery is really on the team (and the Product Owner), not the scrum master. But "delivery manager" is more about the business and meeting the business's needs.

Delivery manager has a more established and clear career path, scrum master kinda doesn't.

- Less facilitation, the team do it themselves more
- Becomes less servant leadership and more coordination and direction, possibly line management
- Shit umbrella gets bigger and wider, but/and more useful/powerful
- Much less focus on agile stuff and more reporting and metrics and things
