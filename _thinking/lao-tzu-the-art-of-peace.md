## Raw notes

### Introduction

- firmly yielding
- It champions peace, flexibility, scepticism, modesty, natural living, relaxation, amiability, innocence, and sincerity.
- This translation focuses on its personal philosophy of 'self-improvement', rather than its deep mysticism
- I do what no other translation I have met does (although Ursula Le Guin comes close): I interpret these opening words not as a description of some deep and elusive feature of the world, but as a piece of advice to those of us who are trying to find our way in this world.
- ... by translating *wu wei* not as "no action" but as "taking it easy"

### One

> If we can set aside out desires<br>
> we may glimpse the underlying mysteries,<br>
> Whereas if we follow out desires<br>
> we notice only the boundaries.

### Two

> Yes, what exists and what does not exist<br>
> produce one another,

### Four

> The way is so deep that it seems to preserve us all.

### Seven

> The sky endures and the earth is long-lasting.<br>
> They can last and endure<br>
> because they do not live for themselves:<br>
> This is why they can endure.

### Nine

> To hold on unduly<br>
> is not as good as letting go.

### Fourteen

> Keep to ancient ways<br>
> and so direct to what is happening today.<br>
> If we understand these ancient beginnings<br>
> we know the patterns of the way.

### Fifteen

> Which of us are able, like muddy water,<br>
> slowly to become clear by stillness?

### Eighteen

> When our social fabric is benighted and disordered<br>
> earnest managers take over.

### Twenty-three

> If even nature cannot make things last very long,<br>
> what makes us think that we can?

### Twenty-six

> Calm is the master of agitation

### Twenty-seven

> Those who do not treasure their teachers<br>
> or care for their students<br>
> are greatly confused, however knowledgable.

### Twenty-eight

> When simplicity is lost<br>
> cunning gadgets appear.<br>
> But when wise people use simple means<br>
> it enhances their work.

### Twenty-nine

> So, if we are wise<br>
> We will avoid excess,<br>
> avoid extravagance,<br>
> avoid arrogance.

### Thirty

> So, if we are good we will be resolute,<br>
> but that is all:<br>
> do not dare to use force;<br>
> Be resolute but not boastful,<br>
> resolute but not overbearing,<br>
> resolute but not arrogant,<br>
> resolute but not complacent,<br>
> resolute but not violent.<br>

### Thirty-two

> A comparison: we make our way in the world<br>
> as small streams flow into great rivers and oceans.

### Thirty-five

> Look for it - it is invisible,<br>
> Listen for it - it cannot be heard,<br>
> But put it into practice - it is inexhaustible.

### Thirty-six

> To seize something<br>
> first contribute to it.

### Thirty-nine

> Let us try not to be exquisite, like jewels,<br>
> But commonplace, like stones.

### Forty-three

> The softest things in the world<br>
> override the hardest things,<br>
> The least substantial<br>
> enter where there is no space.<br>
> This shows us the benefit of not striving.

### Forty-six

> Yes: if we are content with enough<br>
> there will always be enough.

### Forty-nine

> Those who are good - <br>
> let us be good to them;<br>
> Those who are not good - <br>
> let us be good to them as well;<br>
> this way we all gain in goodness.<br>
> ...<br>
> If we are wise<br>
> we live in the world drawing things together;<br>
> For the sake of the world we keep our mind simple.<br>
> Even if people fix their eyes and ears upon us<br>
> if we are wise we become their children.

### Fifty-one

> Give them life but do not hold on to them,<br>
> Assist them but do not claim them,<br>
> Rear them but do not dominate them.<br>
> This is our most profound power.

### Fifty-two

> Noticing the small is real enlightenment;<br>
> Remaining flexible is real strength.

### Fifty-seven

> Be straightforward in governing,<br>
> Be unconventional in warfare.<br>
> And win over the world by not meddling.

### Fifty-eight

> Be upright but do not harm,<br>
> Be candid but not tactless,<br>
> Light the way but do not dazzle.

### Sixty-three

> Contrive to be easygoing, work at not working,<br>
> and savour the flavourless;<br>
> Exalt the small, increase the few,<br>
> and repay injury with kindness.<br>
> ...<br>
> So, if we are wise, we will treat things as difficult;<br>
> That way we will be free of difficulties.

### Sixty-seven

> I have three great treasures<br>
> which I hold on to and cherish:<br>
> The first is kindness,<br>
> The second is thrift,<br>
> The third is deference.

### Sixty-nine

> Yes, when armies do engage<br>
> it is the side with compassion that prevails.
