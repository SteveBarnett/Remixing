## Raw notes


In the T’ai Chi Classics, there’s a line my teacher often quotes:

> (So light an object as) a feather cannot be placed, and
> (so small an object as) a fly cannot alight
> on any part of the body.

The implication is that the tiniest weight can be felt and will set the body in motion. Such exquisite sensitivity means that aggression has nowhere to land on us, so long as we remain connected, aware and free to move.

Each day, I get up and practice and I attempt to be in tune with the seasons but not in step with the times. ... I remind myself that the world of ‘what is broadcast’ differs vastly from what is only spoken of in person, or what is held dearly in people’s hearts. This simple act of remembering helps stabilise me greatly.

### Three sources of uncontrived action

- **Sovereignty.** The sense of self-possession, wisdom gained through life experience, including through initiation, then acting in accordance with this, instead of placating power, allowing oneself to be dominated, or dominating others.
- **Stillness.** This state of being (but while awake) is a vital first step in cultivating the softness at the core of uncontrived action. In T’ai Chi, we call this magnetic peacefulness ‘attracting to emptiness’.
- **Sensing.** Without true sensing, we cannot discern what is happening and so our responses will be off-kilter, we may react and lash out, we may act-out past conditioning and inflict ourselves on others. This is as common at the nation state level as the interpersonal level.

### Additional notes from [It's Just Dogs Barking](https://carolineross.substack.com/p/its-just-dogs-barking)

As with all good knotty questions and hearth-riddles, this is not a problem with a simple solution, it’s a predicament which must be lived through.

“Don’t wrestle with a pig. You’ll both get dirty, and the pig will like it.”

In T’ai Chi we would say ‘don’t enter the competition’,

‘stay lightly connected to the other person’s struggle’.13 This has been one of the greatest instructions for how we can resist bad actors in the world and yet not find ourselves joining in inadvertently by using force. For me, it is at the heart of my Hearth-riddle.

‘relax’ (the first, last and hardest instruction of all).

It is about understanding power, transforming it and our situation via connection, attention and the ability to adapt in a heartbeat. The side-effect? Our relationships are also transformed as we can more clearly see when we are reactive, embattled or frozen, and then hopefully soften so that other options than digging-in are available to us.
