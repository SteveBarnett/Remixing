## Raw notes

> Only staying active will make you want to live a hundred years<br>– Japanese proverb

- Translates roughly as "the happiness of always being busy"
- Finding our ikigai requires a patient search. The intersection of
    - What you love
    - What the world needs
    - What you can be paid for
    - What you are good at
- Presented with new information, the brain creates new connections and is revitalised. This is why it's so important to expose yourself to change, ever if stepping outside of your comfort zone means feeling a bit of anxiety.
- One study found that the people who live the longest have two dispositional traits in common: a positive attitude and a high degree of emotional awareness.
- In (Victor) Frankly's view, there is no need to see [existential] frustration as an anomaly or a symptom of neurosis; instead, it can be a positive thing–a catalyst for change.
- Based on his experience, Frankly believed that our health depends on that natural tension that comes from comparing what we've accomplished so far with what we'd like to achieve in the future.
- The basic principles of Morita therapy
    1. Accept your feelings
    2. Do what you should be doing
        - Focus on the present moment, not trying to eliminate symptoms
    3. Discover your life's purpose
- The seven conditions for achieving Flow
    1. Knowing what to do
    2. Knowing how to do it
    3. Knowing how well you are doing
    4. Knowing where to go
    5. Perceiving significant challenges
    6. Perceiving significant skills
    7. Being free from distractions
- Strategies
    1. Choose a difficult task (but not too difficult!)
    2. Have a clear, concrete, objective
        - It's much more important to have a compass pointing to a concrete objective that to have a map
            - SB: you make the map as you go along, recording where you've been and what you've learned
    3. Concentrate on a single task
        - When we continually ask our brains to switch back and forth between tasks, we waste time, make more mistakes, and remember less of what we've done
- Using flow to find your ikigai
    - What do the activities that drive you to flow have in common?
    - Why do these activities drive you to flow?
- Hara hachi bu
- Studies from the Blue Zones suggest that the people who live longest are not the ones who do the most exercise but rather the ones who move the most.
- The ten rules of ikigai
    1. Stay active; don't retire
    2. Take it slow
    3. Don't fill your stomach
    4. Surround yourself with good friends
    5. Get in shape for your next birthday
    6. Smile
    7. Reconnect with nature
    8. Give thanks
    9. Live in the moment
    10. Follow your ikigai
