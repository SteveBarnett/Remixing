## My own notes, healthy work

- I love my job, but my life outside of work is just as important to me. The balance and the contrast is what keeps me happy, healthy, and excited about the next project.
- I'd much rather put in fewer, higher quality, hours than lots of hours at steadily declining quality and productivity.
- **Crab claw: we need both big and small solutions**
- **MVP it**: make the complex, large scale, long-term, solution in an iterative manner. This lets us take small risks and do small experiments. We can make small mistakes and fix them more easily. We’ll learn more from trial and error than from sitting and thinking.
- Some markers for good actions
    - **make things better by subtraction rather than addition**
    - is something that people enjoy doing
    - have built-in redundancies
    - build rather than attack
    - focus on what connects people rather than what divides people
    - are simple and practical
    - **focus on strategies rather than issues**
- **what low value things can be stopped or delegate**
- **what high value and high time things can be made faster, simpler, or automated**
- **Focus on finishing work, not starting work.**
- **Ask “What’s slowing you down?”**
- take the long-term view
- **look at a range of possible outcomes**
- **routines and rituals** > hoping and thinking 
- focus on tangible, substantial, returns
- have public, accountable, progress reports
- **Retrospectives are the best thing you can do to improve**. They’re the best way to learn about the team, the processes, and more. I think they’re the most important agile activity.
- I prefer counting longer periods of time when I can: **by day instead of by hour**. This helps me focus on outcomes instead of hours, and helps me even out my performance.
    - Counting days instead of hours helps me focus on the work, not the clock. Counting days instead of hours helps me take a longer term view. It smooths out the bumps and lets me look at the big picture.
- be more careful, be more human-centred
- One of my favourite simple ways of helping teams be less busy is to **limit work in progress**.
    - Having less WIP gives us slack, space, room to think and breathe. 
- [FIT: Finishing; Impact; Type](https://human-centred.nz/2023/11/13/how-i-do-my-best-work/)
    - Finishing
        - **Limiting Work In Progress**
        - **Smoothly flowing working with quick reviews**
        - Quick and lean processes in the team and the wider organisation
    - Impact
        - Working closely with clients and with people doing the work
        - **Understanding how the work ties into strategic goals**
            - (What's happened to the pillars stuff?)
        - Seeing action coming from advice
- Why can’t it be both?
- **The values that drive my work**
    - When working with people, especially on accessibility things, I try my best to: be **empathetic**; make it **simple**; be **positive**.

## Slow Productivity

- Principles
    - Do fewer things
        - **our brains work better when we’re not rushing**
        - **Pull Instead of Push**
        - Avoid Task Engines - weekly requests, questions, or small chores a project generates
        - **Put Tasks On Autopilot - specific type of task at the same times on the same days**
    - Work at a natural pace
        - **Make A Five-Year Plan**
        - Simplify Your Workday
            - **reduce the number of tasks you schedule**
            - **reduce the number of appointments on your calendar**
        - Schedule Slow Seasons
        - Implement “Small Seasonality”
            - No Meeting Mondays
    - Obsess over quality
        - **Once you commit to doing something very well, busyness becomes intolerable**
        - Improve Your Taste
            - When you study an unrelated field, the pressure is reduced, and you can approach the topic with a more playful openness.
- **Watch for Pseudo-Productivity**: the use of visible activity as the primary means of approximating actual productive effort.
- The Slowness Revolution
    - **focus on alternatives to what’s wrong**
    - **draw these solutions from time-tested traditions**
