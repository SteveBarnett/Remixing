Notes from "Tao Te Ching – A New Translation" by David Bentley Hart.

## Introduction

- ... **a power that gives rather than takes**, that is inexhaustible precisely because it is untiring in its generosity, self-emptying, even self-impoverishing, yet always full because it is empty of "itself".
- ... neither <span lang="zh">**wu wei**</span> nor <span lang="de">Gelassenheit</span> ("letting go", Meister Eckhart) is merely a matter of passive disengagement; each **is an active ethos whose nature is one of charity and constant concern for others**. One "lets go" of one's self so as to embrace the world.
- ... I have adopted a more authentically Chinese tendency of **not distinguishing so absolutely between the two traditions and of instead holding them together in an oscillating unity** in my mind, with Buddhism constituting a third pole. (And even, when operating in a more Japanese register, with some Shinto thrown in.)
- ... all dogs are natural Daoists of the highest spiritual attainment
- ... <span lang="zh">wu wei</span> means something like "not striving", "inaction" ,"not contending", "refraining from exertion", "without action", or even "letting be."

## Tao Te Ching (Daodejing)

### 1

> Yet **both the hidden and the manifest issue from a single source**,<br>
> **even if we speak as though they were wholly different**

> All we can say of them is that they are an abyss of mystery,<br>
> and even then **a mystery that constantly deepens**

### 9

> Accomplish your task and them immediately withdraw–<br>
> this is the way of Heaven.

### 11

> Thus, while what is tangible may bring advantage,<br>
> it is only by way of emptiness that it renders service.

### 13

> The reason I suffer is that<br>
> I mistake myself for my body.<br>
> When I know my true self to be bodiless,<br>
> I bear my affliction with equanimity.

### 15

> **So very yielding, like ice beginning to thaw**

### 19

> The greater truths to hold on to are these:<br>
> **manifest simplicity**,<br>
> embrace unpretentious purity,<br>
> throw off all self-interest,<br>
> **temper all desires**.

### 26

> **A deep and strong foundation upholds the light and fragile**,<br>
> **a deep tranquility underlies the restless stirring of things**.

### 30

> **What waxes by force soon wanes**.<br>
> This is not in accordance with the Way,<br>
> and whatever does not follow the Way soon comes to ruin.

### 36

> Only what has first expanded itself<br>
> can be diminished;<br>
> only what has first fortified itself<br>
> can be weakened;<br>
> only what has first exalted itself<br>
> can be thrown down;<br>
> only what has first enriched itself<br>
> can be despoiled.

### 40

> The motion of the Way is one of return<br>
> the working of the Way is one of yielding.

### 44

> Whoever has the most lavish desires is the most profligate;<br>
> whoever hoards the greatest wealth will suffer the most grevious loss.<br>
> Whoever is contented with what is sufficient will never know disgrace<br>
> and whoever knows restraint is free from peril<br>
> and so can long endure.

### 46

> There is no calamity greater than discontent<br>
> nor any calamity more dire than a longing for wealth.<br>
> Whoever is satisfied with having simply enough<br>
> will remain always contented.

### 59

> To govern the people and serve Heaven,<br>
> nothing is more needful than restraint.<br>
> Truly, only by restraint is anything quickly acquired,<br>
> but this is done through a deep reserve of Power<br>
> accumulated over a long time.<br>

> The name for this is having deep roots and a sturdy trunk–<br>
> the Way of longevity and of eternal vision.

### 63

> **One who is wise and holy accepts hardships**<br>
> **and so, in the end, finds them to be no hardships at all.**

### 64

> **Peace can be preserved quite easily**<br>
> **because what has not yet happened is easily prevented.**

### 66

> The seas ans rivers are the lords of a hundred great valleys<br>
> because they wisely abase themselves, taking the lower place,<br>
> and in this way become lords of the valleys.

### 67

> Loves makes on capable of courage,<br>
> **restraints makes one capable of generosity**,<br>
> **humility makes one capable of leading**<br>
> and therefore of being shaped into a perfect vessel.

### 72

> **Thus one who is wise and holy**<br>
> **knows himself but never celebrates himself**,<br>
> **cherishes himself but never promotes himself**

### 76

> Thus the stiff and rigid and disciples of death,<br>
> the yielding and tender disciples of life.

### 81

> The wise and holy person does not hoard posessions.<br>
> **The more he acts for others, the more he himself has**;<br>
> **the more he gives to others, the greater his abundace**.
