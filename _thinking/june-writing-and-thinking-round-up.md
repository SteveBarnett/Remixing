Something I'm giving a go is a monthly writing round-up. Writing a bit rather than just gathering notes.

(This didn't quite do what I wanted: what's my remix of what I did this month? What's the thread that ties it all together? I might revisit it.)

[June's stuff](/2026/06/) was quite broad. I didn't read too much, but I did read one practice book and one anti-AI book. 

[Tara Brach's new one, a workbook](/thinking/the-courageous-heart-workbook/), was good. Much like Brené Brown, I find each book tends to be an iteration and improvement on the last one, plus some new bits. It reminded me of my [Mirror metta](/thinking/my-mirror-metta/): I find it difficult to do self-kindness, but receiving it as a reflection seems to work. It was also a good reminder to share personal stories (instead of abstract, conceptual, arguments). Keep it grounded.

[Cory Doctorow's new one on AI](/thinking/ai/the-reverse-centaurs-guide-to-life-after-ai/) was, of course, great. I'm still digesting it. One big thing from there is feeling riled up again about unionising and organising. "Switch from fighting technologies to fighting bosses":  oof! Another was a good reminder to look at the power relationships: who it does it for, and who it does it to. Also oof!

I had a flash on inspiration (after remembering that this is how I work, and Cory Doctorow and Austin Kleon and others have talked about it being how they work) that [Turning flow into stock](/thinking/turning-stock-into-flow/) is A Good Thing. So now it's my theme for this month. Incremental additions and building blocks.

I'm still really finding the [VHIP gates](/themes/2026/#gates-vhip) helpful. It helps make things more concrete for me, and tether my flappy energy to something specific.

I'm finding the [one value, care](/themes/2026/#valued-action-care), a bit tricky. Sort of like it's harder to remember that three or four. Which is weird. Or maybe I'm not finding it clear enough compared to something like Kind. I'll keep an eye on it. I kinda like committing to it for a year, though: see how it goes over a whole stretch.
