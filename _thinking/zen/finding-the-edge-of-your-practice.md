## Raw notes

### What Your Practice Is Not, and What It Is

Ultimately, your practice edge isn’t a problem to be fixed, it’s where your vital life question arises. At its most fundamental, this is a wordless question, an attitude of wonder, a sense of lively tension.

When you’re really in touch with your edge, you experience a sense of urgency. Not a panicked urgency, but one that brings energy and attention to the unfolding of your life.

### Why Is It Valuable to Stay in Touch with Your Edge?

I can’t think of any other way for you to sustain your bodhicitta, or the mind that seeks the way.

Another valuable thing about staying in touch with your edge is getting comfortable with any associated feelings of humility and vulnerability. You’re actually less vulnerable when you become aware of your “perimeter of growth,” but at first it may not feel like it.

### The First Step in Finding a Practice Edge

It’s in zazen that you make contact with your wordless question, or in moments of pure awareness off the meditation seat that are made more likely by zazen practice. 

Looking for what is un-Buddha-like in your mind, body, and behavior isn’t about holding on to some ideal and beating yourself up for falling short, it’s about identifying your growth points.

My teachers often shared a saying, “First you practice for the sake of your own suffering. Then you practice for the sake of others. Finally, you practice for the sake of the Dharma.”

### Your Practice Edge When You Are Suffering

With that disclaimer, then: When you practice with an aspect of your suffering, you focus on insight rather than karma. Again, you are simultaneously taking care of your karma

As you explore your suffering through the lens of practice, you’ll need to engage it as a koan. Just because you identify a situation or even gain some insight into it doesn’t mean your suffering is immediately resolved. Usually, your suffering is alleviated a little bit at a time. You gain some insight, practice letting go, and taste a bit of liberation. Then your karma or situation of ongoing suffering takes over again.
