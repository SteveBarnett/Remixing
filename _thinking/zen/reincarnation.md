## Raw notes

### Introduction

- The reality is that any concept we or others have of ourselves is just a concept.
- I prefer the word continuation to the word reincarnation. There is nothing that leaves something and enters something else–continuation is constantly taking place.

### Chapter One: Karma and Continuation

- We must see that a person is both within and beyond the boundaries of skandhas, just as we see that the tea is not only in the teapot: it is also in the teacup, the person drinking the tea, and the poems and calligraphy they create.
- All of these–karma of thought, speech, and action–are us. We are the totality of our karma.
- As our practice deepens, we're better equipped to handle unwholesome inputs.
- If we know how to handle our thoughts, speech, and action, we will continue to create happiness in ourselves, each other, and the world, even when our body is no longer present in its current form.
- Right View is the foundation and fruit of our practice. Mindfulness leads to concentration and concentration leads to insight, or Right View. Right View os the the view that transcends all views.

### Chapter Two: Who is Reborn?

- The Buddha had the insight that even though we tend to create a concept of self, if we look deeply into our own experience of the world, we find nothing there to call a distinct "self" at all.
- If we really practice it, the teaching of non-self can lead to liberation. Yet it's important not to get caught in the idea of nonself. We should treat the Buddha's teachings as medicine, not as dogma.
- The three Dharma seals should not be looked upon as a description of reality only. They should be looked upon as instruments we can use to train ourselves in looking and in seeing things.
- When conditions are sufficient, there is action. When those conditions cease, action ceases. There is no self involved.
- Because of this strong sense of self, when we learn about nonself, we think there's something–ego, self–to destroy or get rid of. But that's incorrect. A better way to overcome our attachment to a self is to stop feeling the notion of separateness in our thinking.
- Our fear of dying born from our survival instinct nourishes our belief in a separate self.
- The preferences–likes and dislikes–we attach ourselves to play a big role in how we get caught in a notion of a self.
- When we compare life and death, thinking that life is good and death is bad, our fear of death and our survival instinct become ever more activated; we cling to life, we struggle, and we suffer.
- We tend to think, when a person does something, that the person and the action and separate from one another. ... the idea of a separate, permanent actor who becomes the experiencer of the act cannot be observed in our actual experience.
- When we light a matchstick, the flame that manifests consumes the carbon within the matchstick. No one would consider the charred remains of the matchstick to the same as the matchstick prior to lightning. There is no stable "actor/experiencer" here. It would be a waste of time to treat the charred matchstick like an unlit one and try to get fire from it. ... And yet, it's quite common to believe that we have an unchanging self, to believe that the person who produces an action isn't fundamentally changed by the action itself.
- When conditions are sufficient, things manifest. When conditions are no longer sufficient, they stop manifesting in one form in order to manifest in other forms.
- In Buddhism, we chant not to pray but to realise that the so-called "I am" is no longer there.
- Whenever we compare ourselves to others–that we are better or worse, or the same as them–suffering follows. Freedom from comparing leads to happiness right away.

### Chapter Three: Our Many Bodies

- When we look deeply, we realise that our body is far more complex and mysterious than we typically assume.
- It helps us to realise that what we consider a body is simply a concept, one that co-arises with our concept of "outside the body".
- When we sit, we sit mindfully so that we can enjoy our sitting. When we walk, we walk mindfully so that we can enjoy every step.
- The term "body outside the body" means that our body is there outside the physical body. If we can see ourselves outside of ourselves, outside of this body, we begin to see our true body; we transcend notions like being and nonbeing, existing or not existing.
- Without the sunshine, for example, there would be no life on Earth, so you are made of sunshine.

### Chapter Four: Stores Consciousness, Ripening, Rebirth

- there's only continuation–even at the level of our mind, there's no coming from nonbeing into being or vice versa. ... Everything that manifests in our mind does so from causes and conditions as part of an ongoing cycle.
- There are causes and conditions for everything, including the qualities we take to be integral to who we are.
- The teaching on transmission helps us become free from this artificial boundary we create around who we think we are.
- Every action from our ancestors has "perfumed", or influenced, the seeds in our consciousness.
- No seed in our consciousness is one hundred percent innate or one hundred percent transmitted. ... The distinction ... is artificial.
- These distinctions are established in order to help us better understand on an intellectual level seemingly opposite concepts so that we can work with them in our practice.
- There is no manifestation of a phenomenon that is purely individual or purely collective.
- Subject and object co-arise together.

### Chapter Five: Do We Know How to Live?

- We don't need to practice intensely, with a lot of effort, just regularly and diligently.
- The practice should be enjoyable; we should practice diligently, but with ease.
- Right Diligence
    - Try not to allow unbeneficial seeds to manifest
    - If it's already manifested, help it go back down to store consciousness
    - Invite beneficial seeds to come up from store consciousness
    - When something benefical arises, keep it there as long as possible
