Notes from a reread of [Trust in Awakening by Stephen Snyder](https://awakeningdharma.org/book/trust-in-awakening/).

## Raw notes

### Preface

- This poem gives subtle pointers, then removes the concepts from these pointers, leaving the direct knowing of truth.

### I.

> The Great Way is effortless<br>
> With no preferences<br>
> Surrender desire and aversion<br>
> Clarity dazzles

- Preferences derive from our internal self-talks of "I like this" and "I do not like that." **Our preferences support our customary self-identity.** Only when we drop preferences, drop like and dislike, can we see the Great Way before us.
- **By surrendering desire and aversion, we allow what is unfolding in our life to be here while viewing it as exactly right for this moment. We can be with the natural flow of reality.**

## II.

- If the awakening–called <span alng="jp">kenshō</span> in Zen parlance–is short in duration, the normal sense of a me is temporarily displaced. The sense of a me later reappears. It is not thoroughly believed, however.

### III.

- **When we hold tight to our likes and dislikes, we eliminate other possibilities.**
- Our customary self-identity is composed in part by the aggregation of our likes and dislikes. As we release and put down our likes and dislikes, **we become open to a variety of choices** while untethering our sense of self from fixed opinions.

### IV.

> Preserve preferences<br>
> Cloak the wisdom eye

- For most people, **our sense of self depends on generating habitual thoughts, concepts, and closely held opinions to reaffirm constantly who we take our selves to be.** **Maintaining this separate self is psychologically consuming and deeply exhausting.**
- I use the word "Absence" over the customary word choices in Buddhism of "emptiness." "Emptiness" suggest nothing is there, "Absence" suggests something can be there, but is not in this particular moment.
- **When we maintain and preserve our preferences, we are confirming what we like and dislike as a foundation for our customary sense of self.**

### VI.

> Regarding the wordly as authentic<br>
> Obscure reality

### VII.

- Acceptance is an important function on the spiritual path. 

### X.

> Residing in wisdom<br>
> Quiet confidence

- There is no end to realisations and no end to unconscious personal material needing to be engaged, excised, and liberated.

### XV.

- [Diamondlike clarity] refers to our experience of multifaceted knowing.

### XVI.

- Awakening experiences open our consciousness to the multilayered reality of life.
- Should we wish to be seen by others as holy or wise, it usually indicates we are neither.
- **When we truly know through direct experience what we genuinely are, we know simultaneously what everyone is.**
