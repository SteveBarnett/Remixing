Notes from [Decisive by the Heath brothers](https://heathbrothers.com/books/decisive/). [Index card version](/notes/heath-bros/decisive.jpg).

## Widen your options

Break out of a narrow frame.

- Run the "Vanishing options" test
- Expand your options
    - Find someone with broader knowledge
- Consider opportunity cost
    - Yardstick to measure cost of time or money
- Multitrack: AND not OR
    - Multiple paths at once? Embrace one, sample another?
    - Compare and contrast ranging options. Cobble together?
- Toggle between prevention and promotion mindsets
    - Sudden windfall or cutback
- Find someone who has solved your problem

## Reality-test your assumptions

Fight confirmation bias, gather info you can trust.

- Consider the opposite
    - Sparks new info to gather
- Spark constructive disagreement
    - Find a trusted person to disagree with you
- Ask disconfirming questions
- Zoom out
    - Respect the base rates
    - Experience > Intuition
    - Find data that's right in front of you
- Zoom in
    - Take a close-up
    - Field trip
- Ooch
    - Sample the options
    - Lower the barrier to entry a bit
    - Run an experiment. Don't predict when you can know.

## Attain some distance before deciding

Resist short-term emotions, base it on core priorities.

- Try 10/10/10
    - Do it with a friend
    - Short- and long-term implications
- Shift perspectives to gain distance
    - What would I tell my best friend to do?
- Identify and enshrine core priorities
    - A rule to remind you?
- For an agonising decision, gather more options or info
- Fight "status quo bias"
    - (mere exposure + loss aversion)


## Prepare to be wrong

Plan for good and bad outcomes.

- Create a "realistic job preview"
    - Someone who knows the problem
- Run a premortem and preparade, separately
- Set a tripwire
    - Check-in, refocus
- Bookend the future
    - The future is a spectrum
    - What are the top 3 factors influencing the top or bottom end
- Use a safety factor
    - Things don't go smooth or quickly
