## Raw notes

### Front matter

- sangha - a community of lifelong friends supporting one another on our journey
- the immediacy of embodied experience, drawing on natural patterns such as the seasons to illudtrate the cyclical nature of the four tasks
- Central to the teachings was the concept of **care as the unifying principle of the hours tasks** and the 32 dimensions. Through this commitment to care, we cultivate **a life of meaning, connection, and growth.**

### Module 1: Embrace life

#### Introduction to Module 1

- **the willingness to be shaped by what we meet**
- **with a willingness to meet what is here**
- The Four Resolves help shape this territory. They teach us to **prevent harm where we can**, to **recognise and release what contracts us**, to cultivate conditions that support well-being, and to sustain what strengthens us over time.
- Practice becomes attunement–**the courage to respond without securing the outcome.**

#### Chapter 1: Four foundations of mindfulness – the body and feeling tone

- ... when we come back, we don't just come back to the breath, we come back to the whole moment
- **When you sit in meditation you will notice that you don't often have a thought that you haven't had before.**
- Mindfulness of tonality
- Often with tonality, we don't realise it until it's gone.
- ... mindfulness of tonality is about becoming more aware at the beginning that at the end.

#### Chapter 2: The four great efforts – the four resolves

1. Sustain positive states once they have arisen
2. Enable positive states to arise that have not yet arisen
3. Let go of harmful state once they have arisen
4. Prevent harmful states that have not yet arisen from arising

- Creativity, playing
- ... **when we experience a quiet and clear state, the only thing we need to do is just to be with it and actually not do anything.**
- At some point, it will pass, but we can at least be aware of it and enjoy it while it lasts.
- The body can be helpful in terms of grounding.
- **When you come in from work and you're feeling really overwhelmed, you can focus on the feet, or your hands.**
- Listening to the sound of the world, without picking or choosing, really having this wide, receptive awareness.
- Questioning really helps us to develop a grounded brightness, or grounded flexibility.
- **When we look at letting go, ... it's useful to note that we have to see that we've been triggered by something that put us into that state.**
- Four types of letting go: after, during, at the beginning, before
- **it's important that we don't equate letting go with stopping**
- ... conditionality helps us see that it's a combination of so many different things
- **To prevent unskilful states before they arise, we look at the contributing factors**

#### Chapter 3: A democracy of the imagination

- the four tasks are for **the real world, in which we continually confront problems that challenge our resolve**
- ... suffering is not a problem to be solved – to be 'eradicated', as tradition has it. Rather, it's a source of transformative power. It's to be embraced, and in the process, creatively transformed into a path, a way of life.
- The four steps of creativity: desire, energy, intuition (citta, mind, heart-mind), experimentation.

#### Chapter 4: Creative awareness

- **anchoring and exploring, ... cultivating brightness and calmness.**
- **acceptance ... "to see and know things as they come to be"**
- **"Be alive, be present to what occurs"**
- "How can we flourish all together in the world?"

#### Chapter 5: A jazz musician walks into a meditation room...

- While I cannot escape mistakes, I *can* trust that I will *recognise* them and hopefully learn from that.
- ... recognise a mistake as such, and setting an intention to do things differently in the future.
- **My way out of tension was to experiment**

### Module 2: Let reactivity be

- **courage (energy) as the willingness to remain with discomfort long enough to enough to learn from it**
- **we do not deny anger, anxiety, or hurt – but we stop tightening around them**

#### Chapter 6: The dynamics of care

- Sometimes as one proceeds, it makes more sense to move horizontally, sometimes vertically
- [The four Noble Truths are] 'true' in the sense of ... 'clearly visible'. ... facts ...
- **When we start looking into our own minds, into our reactive patterns, we realise how little control we have other them**
- The point isn't to understand where they come from, but that they do come
- **Reactivity is most insidious in the little things we do all the time**
- Creativity is intrinsically as aspect of care, the aspect whereby our care finds expression and form

#### Chapter 7: Cultivating the five powers in meditation

- After, in the middle, at the beginning, before
- **In the middle – bodily awareness that we are experiencing reactivity, alongside 'selfing'**
- The second stage **is the most difficult one**, and this is the one which happens when we cultivate mindfulness
- ... **when you become aware of it, what goes it the enjoyment and the justification**
- ... this does not stop these thoughts, but it does diminish their power
- **At the beginning – awareness of underlying conditions**
- ... it's recognising: what is going to help me? What is not going to help me? And what are the different things I can do? You can look at this either as prevention or creative engagement.
- **""Maybe this time I'll try something different"**

#### Chapter 8: Confident and focussed - an overview of the five powers

- **the Buddha recommended finding ease and comfort and it helps release our reactivity**
- Even if it's just an appreciation for **a slightly less reactive state, dwelling in it gladly, enjoying the relief**, helps a lot in letting any present reactivity alone, and what that letting go there is more **non-reactivity to delight in**.

#### Chapter 9: Māra

- ... approaching reactivity in a personified way can help us work it *as* persons from within our actual lives in all their complexity
- Māra offers us a story rather than a theory, a narrative frame of imagery, legend, and myth with serves as a deeper resources for meeting reactivity than a view of our lives as a series of positive and negative states.
- ... **the Buddha conquers Māra** not by getting rid of him, but rather **by learning to recognise him** – to recognise what is happening when the destructive thoughts, emotions and habits that Māra represents invade the heart and mind.
- We free ourselves not by literally destroying these but by understanding what they are and what they're doing as they unfold
- It helps us to accept that this reactive aspect of ourselves is part of what it means to be alive.
- ... what **Nagarjuna** meant when we **wrote that samsara and nirvana as being one**. He doesn't mean that they are the same, as he is often misunderstood to be saying. Rather, he says **that they have the same horizon, that they occupy the same space.**

#### Chapter 10: The fire of reactivity

- *upādāna* (very-opposite-of-generosity) - clinging
- providing fuel for a fire: adding logs, fanning the flames
- Since **reactivity** has a survival instinct, it will grab hold of whatever our memories of fantasies can offer so it can keep alive and feeling justified. It **ends up feeding on things that possibly have no relationship whatsoever with what first triggered that irritation** ...
- **take away the fuel and stop it**
- The fuel
- By **finding ways to ease and relax the body, we can take away at least some of the power**. The same can be said of **speed and busyness and so just by slowing down you can already lower the heat**.
- **A lot of the time the problem with letting go is that it suggests you have to 'make it stop.'**
- **The task is not to force anything to cease, only to withdraw your contribution to reactivity, to discontinue feeding the fire.**
- ... to stay with the feeling and not go with the story.
- Another major fuelling mechanism is identification, or self-centredness
