## Rhetorical Patterns

- Disciplinary/Directive: a grammatical pattern
- Test and Apologize: boundary violation, indicator for future abuse
- Conditional Decency
- Were You at the Capitol on January 6th?: weapons and military signalling
- Is This a Threat?

### Some other ones

- Cult Leader Language
- Looking for a Tradwife: framed as loyalty; can occur with Disciplinary/Directive and Cult Leader Language
- Are You My Mother?: or executive assistant; offloading decisions and logistics
- I'm a Very Busy Man: signals him as priority
- My Kids Come First
- Blue Ribbon for Bare Minimum: wanting big credit for small things
- Bombastic Syntax: scammer or AI flags
- Six Flags or Red Flags?: hyper-masculine bravado

### Also

- using passive voice to dodge accountability
- "I don't do drama" meaning immature conflict handling

## Criticisms

- Irreversibility with no error-correction
- False positives
    - surface linguistic features can be from disability, class, or ESL
    - But/and: smaller risk than giving bad men a chance
- Pool depletion
    - worst in small population
    - Jennie Young doesn't see this as a problem
        - (I agree! Otherwise the implication is that you have to accept a bad man because there's no-one else.)
- Asymmetric standards
