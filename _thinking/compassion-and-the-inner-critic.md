## Gathered notes

- The process: things happens; we judge it; we frame it as a personal flaw
- This patterns separates us from the (direct) experience, as it is
- We learn and grow when we have permission to explore, to make mistakes
- Notice the movement more than the direction of the movement

## Raw notes

- **Something’s happened, it’s been judged, now it’s identified with and framed as a personal flaw.**
- **When this pattern is present, we disconnect from the direct experience** of drowsiness in the moment because we’ve made it wrong and a personal failing. We’ve actually compounded the suffering. We’ve created another loop, another layer. In this whole process **we’ve become disconnected from the experience as it simply is in the moment**.
- I find it helpful to contemplate how we grow and learn. **We grow when we have permission to discover, permission to explore, permission to make mistakes**, just like children are given space to discover, **rather than our experience being something we have got wrong and therefore need to fix**.
- When the bell rings at the end of meditation, we notice how we respond to the bell. **Be curious about this as a movement through, rather than a movement towards or away from pleasant or unpleasant feeling.**
- The inner critic doesn’t simply judge and evaluate the experience. It also puts us down for having this experience.
- In Our Daily Life practice, we can apply the following.
    1. Notice the inner critic
    2. Write a(n objective) description of the situation
    3. Write down the judgment
    4. Notice how the judgment affects you
    5. See if there’s space to allow the original situation. “This is the way it is.”
- We don’t have to make it change or go away. Its nature is to change and go away. If we don’t reject the situation, if we don’t get complicated with it, it simply passes through.
