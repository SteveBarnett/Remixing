### Part 3: Shame, Reactance, and Harm Reduction

- It's better to share the harmful ways of using it, with warnings, rather than a blanket ban or no
- Taking a zero-tolerance approach isn't going to solve the problems, it'll make them worse
- Shaming doesn't work
- Psychological reactance
- **People are less likely to do a harmful thing makes if they know it's harmful**
    - **But telling them they're bad or wrong for doing it makes them (reactfully) use it more**
- **Harm reduction**
    - Reducing harm from an action instead of demonising it
    - **Meeting people where they're at**
    - **Give people the tools to lessen the damage they might do**
    - **Teaching people how to do harmful things more safely has better outcomes than telling them not to do the harmful thing**
- How can we reduce AI harm?
    - How LLMs work, what they can and can't do
    - (citing sources is helpful and important)
    - **Having a grounded and complete understanding (also) means understanding what they can do well**
    - Honesty about both
    - Support or at least tolerate people doing good AI education
    - **Engage with the users without stigmatising them**
- "Some criticisms I anticipate"
    - People are using what they have available (an LLM) to cope with systemic problems
- Address the individual and the systemic scales
- Shame only really works for the shamer - feeling more conforted, feeling more in control
- To address climate anxiety, people take small, local, attainable steps
