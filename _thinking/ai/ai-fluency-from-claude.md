## Raw notes

### Description

- You clearly define the audience for your output
- You specify the format and structure you need
- You communicate the tone and style that fits your voice
- **You break complex tasks into smaller steps for the AI to follow**
    - **Guiding AI through a process step by step helps you maintain control and catch issues early rather than at the end.**
- You build on previous exchanges to refine your work together
- **You provide examples of what good looks like**
    - **Showing AI concrete examples of your expectations communicates more than abstract descriptions ever could.**
- You tell the AI how you want it to interact with you
- You set boundaries on what the AI should and shouldn't do
    - Being clear about limits helps AI focus on what matters most to you and avoid wasted effort.

### Delegation

- **You consult AI about how to approach the problem before diving into execution**
    - Asking AI to help you think through your approach—before starting work—leverages its ability to suggest frameworks, surface considerations, and identify angles you might not have considered.
- **You recognise when a task isn't right for AI**
    - **Knowing when to not use AI is just as important as knowing when to use it.**
    - "This is too personal. I should write this myself."
    - "The stakes are too high to rely on AI for this decision."
    - "Actually, this needs human judgment I can't outsource."
    - "Let me handle this one—it requires relationship context."
    - "I should probably do this manually to make sure it's right."
    - **Consider whether the task involves personal relationships or high-stakes decisions.**
- **You consider what AI does well and where it struggles**
    - **Understanding AI's strengths and limitations helps you set realistic expectations and plan for where you'll need to do extra verification.**
    - **"AI is great at generating options, but I'll need to evaluate them myself."**
    - "This involves recent events, so the information might be outdated."
    - **"I know AI can miss nuance, so I'll review the tone carefully."**
    - **"You're good at structure, but I'll need to fact-check the specifics."**
    - "This requires creativity you might not have—let's see."
    - **"I expect you might hallucinate details here, so I'll verify."**
    - **"You tend to be verbose, so I'll need to trim this down."**
- **You stay involved rather than just accepting what AI produces**
    - Treating AI as a collaborator rather than a vending machine leads to better outcomes and helps you learn.
    - **Ask Claude to explain its reasoning: 'Walk me through how you approached this.**
    - **Request options instead of one answer: 'Give me three different directions to consider.**
- **You choose the right tool or approach for the job**
    - **Different AI tools and techniques work better for different tasks and matching them thoughtfully improves your results.**
- You adjust who does what as the work evolves

### Discernment

- **You check facts and claims that matter**
    - **Verifying important information AI provides protects you from confidently sharing something incorrect.**
- **You notice when AI's reasoning doesn't hold up**
    - **Catching logical gaps or assumptions in how AI arrived at an answer helps you avoid building on a shaky foundation.**
- You recognise when AI might be missing something important
- You assess whether you and IA are making progress together
- You notice when AI's communication style isn't working for you

### Diligence

- **You're mindful about what information you share with AI**
    - **Being thoughtful about the data and context you provide protects privacy and helps you use AI responsibly.**
- **You're honest about AI's role in your work when it matters**
    - **Being upfront about how AI contributed to your work builds trust and meets the expectations of those who rely on you.**
- You take responsibility for what you share or submit
    - Standing behind AI-assisted work as your own means you've verified it meets your standards and you're accountable for it.
- You consider the consequences of putting this work into the world
    - Thinking through how your AI-assisted work might be received or used helps you catch issues before they cause problems.
