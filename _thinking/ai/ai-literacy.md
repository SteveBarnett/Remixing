## Booster-friendly version: how to get the most out of AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude

AI (in this case, meaning mostly LLMs like ChatGPT and Claude) is powerful and can be very useful! It's great at transforming text, converting formats, generating code, and more. But/and there are ways to use it to get the most out of it. Knowing what it can do well and what it can’t do well lets us choose where, when, and how to use it. In particular, using AI can be worthwhile when verifying the output is quicker and easier than producing it ourselves.

### Ask small things, one at a time

AI is better at smaller, clearer, tasks, so break complex things into smaller steps and do them one-by-one. Check the results at each step and steer back on track where necessary

- The quality of the output drops off in big batches or in long conversations.
- AI isn't always good at multi-tasking. Asking it to do several things at once can make all the other problems worse!

### Look for problems, not approval

Ask AI to find holes and mistakes in the work, rather than ask it if we're right.

- AI is fast at generating options. It's less useful for confirmation because it can be sycophantic: it's trained to tell us what we want to hear. Even if we suggest a bad idea, the AI can say it's a good idea, and tell us we're right.
- This can be especially tricky for high-stakes and/or interpersonal topics!

### Use AI where output variation is acceptable

Use AI when we don't need the same output for the same input.

- AI output is probabilistic and non-deterministic: the same input won't always give the same output. That's useful for making variations, but it can mean trouble when we need precision or accuracy!
- Sometimes plain old automation is all we need.

### Tend towards “AI in the loop” more than “human in the loop”

Start with the problem rather than AI as the solution. Do some of the work/thinking without AI first. Use AI where it's stronger: as a question-asker and option-suggester rather than an answer-provider.

- When we start with AI as a solution, we look for problems that AI can solve. Doing the thinking ourselves first helps guard against cognitive atrophy and cognitive offloading.
- AI makes it easier to jump to an answer rather than deeply considering the question and the process to get to the answer. The process is where we learn and make decisions. It's where we're challenged and apply active effort, critical thinking, and analysis.

### Keep private things private

Don't put any sensitive or private information into an AI. Opt out of user-content training. When we can, locally host an AI instead of using a large cloud one.

- The information we put into AI is not always private.
- Researchers are finding new ways to leak data.
- User inputs may be used to train the AI, depending on the product.

### Check the details

Don't treat AI output as factual information: check everything it produces, in fine detail, for accuracy and consistency.

- AI produces plausible text rather than facts. True things are often the most plausible, but not always!
- This leads to "hallucinations": confidently stating false information as true. In particular, AI tends to get specific facts wrong. There has been some progress on this, though.
