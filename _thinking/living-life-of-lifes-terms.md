## Raw notes

### Front matter

- sangha - a community of lifelong friends supporting one another on our journey
- the immediacy of embodied experience, drawing on natural patterns such as the seasons to illudtrate the cyclical nature of the four tasks
- Central to the teachings was the concept of **care as the unifying principle of the hours tasks** and the 32 dimensions. Through this commitment to care, we cultivate **a life of meaning, connection, and growth.**

### Module 1: Embrace life

#### Introduction to Module 1

- **the willingness to be shaped by what we meet**
- **with a willingness to meet what is here**
- The Four Resolves help shape this territory. They teach us to **prevent harm where we can**, to **recognise and release what contracts us**, to cultivate conditions that support well-being, and to sustain what strengthens us over time.
- Practice becomes attunement–**the courage to respond without securing the outcome.**

#### Chapter 1: Four foundations of mindfulness – the body and feeling tone

- ... when we come back, we don't just come back to the breath, we come back to the whole moment
- **When you sit in meditation you will notice that you don't often have a thought that you haven't had before.**
- Mindfulness of tonality
- Often with tonality, we don't realise it until it's gone.
- ... mindfulness of tonality is about becoming more aware at the beginning that at the end.

#### Chapter 2: The four great efforts – the four resolves

1. Sustain positive states once they have arisen
2. Enable positive states to arise that have not yet arisen
3. Let go of harmful state once they have arisen
4. Prevent harmful states that have not yet arisen from arising

- Creativity, playing
- ... **when we experience a quiet and clear state, the only thing we need to do is just to be with it and actually not do anything.**
- At some point, it will pass, but we can at least be aware of it and enjoy it while it lasts.
- The body can be helpful in terms of grounding.
- **When you come in from work and you're feeling really overwhelmed, you can focus on the feet, or your hands.**
- Listening to the sound of the world, without picking or choosing, really having this wide, receptive awareness.
- Questioning really helps us to develop a grounded brightness, or grounded flexibility.
- **When we look at letting go, ... it's useful to note that we have to see that we've been triggered by something that put us into that state.**
- Four types of letting go: after, during, at the beginning, before
- **it's important that we don't equate letting go with stopping**
- ... conditionality helps us see that it's a combination of so many different things
- **To prevent unskilful states before they arise, we look at the contributing factors**

#### Chapter 3: A democracy of the imagination

- the four tasks are for **the real world, in which we continually confront problems that challenge our resolve**
- ... suffering is not a problem to be solved – to be 'eradicated', as tradition has it. Rather, it's a source of transformative power. It's to be embraced, and in the process, creatively transformed into a path, a way of life.
- The four steps of creativity: desire, energy, intuition (citta, mind, heart-mind), experimentation.

#### Chapter 4: Creative awareness

- **anchoring and exploring, ... cultivating brightness and calmness.**
- **acceptance ... "to see and know things as they come to be"**
- **"Be alive, be present to what occurs"**
- "How can we flourish all together in the world?"

#### Chapter 5: A jazz musician walks into a meditation room...

- While I cannot escape mistakes, I *can* trust that I will *recognise* them and hopefully learn from that.
- ... recognise a mistake as such, and setting an intention to do things differently in the future.
- **My way out of tension was to experiment**
