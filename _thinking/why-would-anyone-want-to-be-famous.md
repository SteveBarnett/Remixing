## Raw notes

Why would anyone want to be famous?

Maybe because fame, from the outside, looks like love.

But it isn’t.

Fame is never love, no matter how you feel it, and no matter how much you have of it, and no matter how much it may look like love from the outside.

Fame can’t be love because fame is always a misunderstanding; fame mistakes the relational power of co-creation for a name.

These days, fame is mostly tragic.

Because these days, fame has become a shell people seek for itself.

It’s become too easy to miss the point that honest communication is the real power, to confuse it with ‘getting attention,’ or to become famous and then be unable to get people to communicate honestly with you.

... love is not about metrics. It is about listening, responding, communicating, and in so doing, feeling the earth and sky open into another place.

Love is never located in any one person or time. Love is always in more than one body—even when you’re in communication with yourself, even when you’re alone in your room writing that song or poem—because it’s relational; it’s a communication with more than your current story.

The important distinction is not the name or label, that’s not why they are powerful—they are powerful because they opened a portal of feeling that resonated beyond my story but included me.

The power is always relational: There was some sort of listening, resonating and opening to that channeling of creativity that made me want to give that to others. That’s what happens when we click in with the vibration of nonjudgemental, sensual relation—the power of communication.
