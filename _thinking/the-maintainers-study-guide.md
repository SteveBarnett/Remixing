## Raw notes

A guide to ground your practice in principles of maintenance, repair, and care.

### Introduction

This resource explores starting points to furthering a global society that values maintenance, repair, care, and building better infrastructures.

### Looking at Maintenance in Our World

- Finding awe in the everyday: our interdependent world
    - We inhabit a highly interdependent world.
- Beyond maintenance of the built-world: understanding care infrastructures
    - So much maintenance is grounded in acts of care by individuals: care for the earth, care for each other, care for neighbors.

### Nine Maintenance Fundamentals

We hope these serve as a grounding and jumping off point for those looking to engage with maintenance as a theory and a practice.

1. Our capitalist society rewards, fetishizes, and is fascinated by the “innovative”
    - While innovation is undoubtedly essential (think medical advancements, or scientific discovery), the obsession with novelty leads to ignoring the care of our existing technologies and built environments, which normalizes unsustainable cycles of obsolescence.
2. Maintenance tasks are typically perceived as mundane, and are often undervalued, underappreciated, and unseen.
3. We reclaim maintenance as interesting, powerful, and beautiful.
4. Failures teach us about maintenance.
    - Well-maintained systems, tools, and infrastructure are often invisibilized, and we don’t consider their vulnerability until a challenge arises. 
5. An *Ethics of Care* is a path forward, deciding to maintain infrastructures and objects are implicit acts of care for others.
6. Repair Economies prompt us to fix, reuse, and repair.
7. When we consider the long-term, we cultivate a generational mindset and success for the public good.
8. Understanding lifecycles: not everything needs to last forever.
9. We need an interdisciplinary, intergenerational, and global knowledge exchange to forward discourse and practice around maintenance and care.

### From Theory to Practice

How to implement a maintenance mindset when working on a project

- Appreciate and honor the work before you
- Remember: maintenance, repair and care is a continuum, and it takes time.
- Prioritize and value self-care, and the care of those in your proximity
- Be part of the global repair culture
    - "A maintenance mindset makes you a better complex
systems thinker." 
- What we can learn from system failures and deferred maintenance
- Break down knowledge silos: engage with different fields
- Think long-term, preserve what matters, and understand how to end with care.
- A collective body of knowledge: adapt, iterate and share your own work so others can do the same.
    - How can you consider efforts you’ve already done as a “draft” of a larger project? Iterate and build upon your work and others, with proper accreditation, to serve as functional groundwork for you, or the next person who will pick up the work after you.
- Solidarity and support
