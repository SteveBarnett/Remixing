### How can Buddhists guide AI development and developers? How do we use it? Some invitations, considerations. Some misperceptions.

- AI is a technology, like writing or print. Tech developments over many centuries. There’s always been the question of ethics and bias. In developing and using technology.
- Every decision we make is an ethical decision.
- AI is very powerful because it’s a great mimic.
- The huge amount of human data and the developers design choices.
- Three principles of consideration
    - What the systems are capable of. The algorithms, the data
    - Human capacities, vulnerabilities (psychological, ethical, spiritual)
    - Incentives that are impacting the developers and users - time spent on monetising eyeballs
- Interdependent intelligence
- AI is a mirror of humanity because of the data we’ve been feeding it
- Our ethical compass is influenced by the incentive structures
- Considerations for fairness, lack of bias, compassion, wisdom
- [How to make AI more kind, more benevolent? We can’t! It’s a mirror of us! (eish)]
- We see the bad news, not how hard people are working to make it good, safe, etc
- New technology has always had a social cost, but AI feels more sweeping
    - [Kinda true, but not in the way we tend to think - AI is worse at our jobs but is still taking them]

### Interdependence and AI

- “All AI is evil” is not a wise response
- There’s nothing inherently bad about it [sort of, but… the systems around it are bad]
- Use it as a support, not a replacement
- There is some evidence of cognitive atrophy, similar to other tech
    - [yeah, but it depends, it’s context-sensitive]
- Industrial Revolution: many jobs were replaced by machines
- [Our context, the way AI automation is replacing jobs, is different, the capitalist incentives as different]
- All things are empty and without self, all things arise from the causes and conditions of their interactions, co-creation
