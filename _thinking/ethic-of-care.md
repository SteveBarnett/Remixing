Quick little notes on care, ethic of care, for [2026's theme](/themes/2026/#intro).

---

From Carol Gilligan:

- an ethic of care is grounded in voice, **relationships**, attention, **careful and respectful listening**.
- Inductive, contextual, psychological, logic rather than deductive or mathematical.

Secular Buddhism highlights care, the fundamental ethic of Buddha's teachings.

- There are parallels between Buddhist moral phenomenology and the ethic of care tradition.
- Care as a primary concern, with an emphasis on context and relationships.

Stephen Batchelor's A Cartography of Care

- our corporeality discloses that we are dependent on others, and others on us, to survive and have well-being

> the ethical question is not a one-sided “What ought I do?” but relational “What is the caring response?"

> The feminist ethic of care is a different voice within a patriarchal culture because it joins reason with emotion, mind with body, self with relationships, men with women, resisting the divisions that maintain a patriarchal order (Gilligan, 2011).

- Appamāda - caring, or non-uncaringness

>  **The ethic of care** guides us in acting carefully in the human world and **highlights the costs of carelessness**. It is grounded less in moral precepts than in psychological wisdom, **underscoring the cost of not paying attention, not listening, being absent rather than present, not responding with integrity and respect** . – Gilligan, 2014, p. 103.

- Reactivity arises in relation to the three marks of existence: impermanence, suffering, selflessness.
