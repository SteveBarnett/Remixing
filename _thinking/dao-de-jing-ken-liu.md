### Not quite an introduction

- As a writer myself, I'm keenly aware of the instability of texts and the futility of tracing and enforcing authority. Each of my published pieces of fiction exists in multiple versions and contains alterations made during transmission and publication ... Literally no "authoritative" version of my fiction exists anywhere except in my head.
- ... *authenticity* and *authoritative* are always loaded terms that tell us more about those who would like to claim the power of judgement than about reality.
- Core to his philosophy is the idea that the fool is wiser than the self-proclaimed clever man, that trying to prove a point is never as good as simply living the truth, that striving and contending and vying for dominance are counter-productive–the best way to silence those who doubt you is by not playing their game.
- Trying to justify a perspective, no matter how well-intentioned the effort, is resistance, contention, assertion, aggression, dominance. We may have been taught by modernity that these qualities are always desirable and universally good, but the truth is otherwise.
- A plain translation strives for the Daoist ideal of uncarved wood, eschewing ornamentation and ostentatious craft. A plain translation, however, does not *simplify*.
- The biggest obstacle to full engagement with the *Dao De Jing* is the temptation to rush through. The chapters are so short, so "simple", that the modern reader, trained from toddlerhood to "consume" content, to race to the end of the paragraph before it has scrolled off the screen, to scan for "main ideas" rather than to let each and every word fill the mind, is tempted to finish it in twenty minutes.

### Laozi's life

- Writers and orators developed schools of thought such as Confucianism (emphasis on benevolence and humanism), Legalism (emphasis on efficient administration and realpolitik), Mohism (emphasis on all-encompassing love and empiricism), and many others.

### Pao Ding carving cattle

> Then I stand up straight and look around–there's no feeling better in the world. Quickly, I clean the knife and put it away.
- Pao Ding acknowledges the joy of a task well done–he looks around, proud of his achievement, but only for a moment. Immediately he cleans the knife and puts it away, not just to protect the blade, but also to leave no trace that he has done the deed.

### Mercy and justice

- ... abstract terms draw arbitrary borders around related concepts that blend and merge into one another, and different languages draw such borders differently.
- abstractions gain meaning via concrete applications.
- The two words do not function like the docking ports of two spacecraft, seamlessly mapping one to the other. Rather, they're like two trees that serve similar purposes for their respective communities: marking the entrance to the village for weary travellers, sheltering songbirds and nut-hoarding rodents from the rain, concealing lovers meeting by the moon, shading labourers from the blazing sun.
